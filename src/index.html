📖 Feature Guide

⚠ Your Responsibility as Pilot-in-Command

This tool is a computational aid, not an authoritative source, and it does not replace your own flight planning. You remain solely responsible for every figure you use. Before flight, cross-reference the tool's output against your own data: headings and distances against your current VFR chart and plotter, times and fuel against your own computation and the aircraft's actual POH (the built-in tables assume 3100 lb, recommended lean, and specific climb techniques - your weight and technique will differ), magnetic variation against the chart isogonals, and forecast winds against your official weather briefing. Check terrain and MEF yourself - this tool has no terrain awareness. Apply your compass deviation card to the MH column.

The tool runs an automatic integrity check after every change and shows a red banner if any computed figure is invalid or physically implausible. If that banner appears, or if any number simply looks wrong to you, do not use the output. A figure you have not verified is a figure you should not fly.

🚀 Quick Start

  • Click the map to add a waypoint. Name it in the prompt — the first click is your departure.
  • Type PATTERN as the name — or press the traffic-circuit option — to log circuit laps (time & fuel, no distance). The circuit altitude is filled in from the aerodrome you are at: the published field elevation rounded to the nearest 100 ft, plus 1000 ft — so ENTC's 31 ft gives 1000 ft. ENDU is set to 1500 ft, the figure the school flies. This is a derived default, not a published value — the eAIP gives us elevations, never circuit altitudes, so check the VAC and edit the cell if it differs. More than 5 NM from any aerodrome nothing is derived and the cell is yours to fill.
  • Dialogs are keyboard-driven: Enter takes the highlighted option, 1–9 pick an option directly, Esc cancels. Confirmations that used to be two chained OK/Cancel questions (like saving) are now a single list of every option. Notifications appear as a small note in the corner and fade on their own — no click needed.
  • Drag any waypoint to move it — tracks, distances and magnetic variation recalculate live.
  • Right-click a waypoint to rename or delete it without leaving the map. (A circuit stop can only be deleted: its name is the marker that makes it a circuit.) Right-clicking the line between waypoints opens the leg panel instead — see below.
  • Ctrl+Z undoes and Ctrl+Shift+Z redoes (⌘ on Mac), step by step up to 30 states — same stacks as the Undo/Redo buttons. Inside a text field the keys keep their normal text-editing meaning.
  • Flight plans name themselves after their route — first and last waypoint, e.g. ENDU-ENEV — and the name follows the route as you edit. A plan with fewer than two waypoints shows "Flight Plan N" until the route exists.
  • With several flight plans, only the active one is clickable in Edit Mode — the others fade so a return route can be drawn straight over its outbound (ENDU→ENEV→ENDU) without grabbing the wrong line. Switch flights with the ✏ button on each plan; View Mode shows every flight at full strength.
  • Grab a leg line and drag to bend it around terrain: a diamond via point appears under the cursor and follows it, with the route redrawing as you move — one motion, press to release. Let go where you want it. A plain click still drops a via point on the spot, and either way you can drag the diamond again later or right-click it to remove. The line has a generous invisible grab area, so you do not have to hit the 4-pixel stroke exactly.
  • A via point bends a leg without adding a row. The leg stays ONE row on the OFP; its distance, time and fuel follow the bent path. The row's TT/MT/WCA/MH show the direct line between the two named waypoints (what you measure on the chart between the fixes); the flown per-segment tracks are in the ↳ sub-line under the row and per-segment in the plotting list — when a leg bends, steer by those, not by the row heading.
  • Forgot a waypoint in the middle of a route? Right-click the leg line where you want it: it gets its own OFP row with its own altitude, times and fuel, the leg splits in two, and any via points on it stay on the correct half so the flown track does not move. (Clicking empty map adds a waypoint at the END of the route; right-clicking the line is how you get one in the middle.) The new waypoint inherits the altitude, OAT and wind of the waypoint it is inserted before — the leg was already planned to arrive at those, so nothing is invented — and all of it is editable in the row. Its magnetic variation is computed for the new position.
  • Climb and descent no longer split legs into separate rows: each leg row shows total distance/time/burn for easy copying, with the CLB/DES portion and TOC/TOD marking distance in the sub-line.
  • TOC and TOD are drawn as a short tick across the track, the way you would mark them on the paper chart, with a small TOC / TOD chip beside it — hover the chip for the full sentence (which altitude, how far from which fix, and for a TOD the aerodrome being descended to). The distances are also in the leg's ↳ sub-line and the plotting list, which is what you copy from and what prints. A descent that fills a whole leg starts at the previous fix, and the mark is drawn there and says so.
  • Press 👁 View Mode to lock all editing for in-flight use; tapping a map waypoint then highlights its OFP row. The button flips to ✏ Edit Mode to switch back.
  • Undo / Redo cover every change. Esc closes any window like this one.

⛽ Flight Defaults & Fuel

  • New waypoints inherit the defaults: wind starts at 000/00, OAT follows ISA automatically for your cruise altitude until you type a real temperature.
  • Initial Fuel starts the Rem column; startup/taxi/takeoff burn (POH 1.7 gal) is charged once per mission.
  • Set a Reserve — any leg landing below it turns the Rem cell red.
  • Enter an ETD to get an ETO clock time on every leg and a mission ETA.
  • Saved routes refresh their magnetic variation when loaded. A route saved years ago carries the variation that was current then, so every waypoint is re-computed with today's WMM on load and a note says how many changed. A VAR you typed yourself is treated as a deliberate override and is never touched.
  • Re-saving a route you loaded offers to update it in place ("Update the saved route …?"), so refining a mission does not leave a trail of near-duplicate copies. Decline and it saves as a new entry as before.

🛠 Aircraft Performance (⚙ Settings)

  • C182T mode uses the genuine POH tables (Fig 5-8 / 5-9, 3100 lb): pick your cruise RPM and MP; TAS & fuel flow interpolate on altitude and ISA deviation, and MP auto-caps at full throttle with altitude.
  • On a CLB+CRZ leg the row's TAS (and the WCA/MH derived from it) is the cruise TAS — the slower climb is fully accounted in the leg's time, fuel and GS and detailed in the ↳ sub-line. A leg that is all climb shows the climb TAS. The integrity check still compares wind against the slowest phase.
  • Climb technique: POH Fig 5-8 (full throttle, 90 KIAS, +10% per 10°C above ISA) or Cruise climb with your own observed ROC and fuel flow.
  • Other aircraft: switch to Manual mode and enter your own cruise/climb numbers.
  • Units (NM/km/SM, gal/L/kg) and dark mode live here too — all values convert everywhere when you switch.

💨 Winds

  • Open the Wind Matrix (💨 button, or click any W/V cell) and Tab through Dir → Spd → OAT for every leg without touching the mouse.
  • 🌐 Fetch Forecast Winds pulls live/forecast winds & OAT from Open-Meteo (up to 16 days ahead): per-leg average at your planned altitude, interpolated between pressure levels and forecast hours. Pick a specific model (ECMWF / ICON / GFS) to know exactly what you're flying on, or Compare to fill the three-model mean and see how much the models disagree — big spread = uncertain winds. It only fills the matrix — you review, then Save & Apply. Requires internet.
  • Winds apply per-leg; WCA, MH and GS update instantly.
  • The wind modal's Flight date is the same date as the planner's Flight Date field — they stay in sync, so sun times and fetched winds always describe the same day. If your Flight Date is outside the forecast range (today … +15 days) the modal says so instead of silently fetching a different day.

🌅 Daylight & VFR Day

  • The Daylight & VFR Day card under the mission totals shows sunrise/sunset and the legal day-VFR window at your departure and destination for the Flight Date (defaults to today; deliberately not saved between sessions so a stale date can never show wrong sun times).
  • The rule: SERA Art. 2(97) (Reg. (EU) 923/2012) defines night as the hours between the end of evening civil twilight and the beginning of morning civil twilight — the boundary is the sun's centre 6° below the horizon. Norway applies SERA through BSL F 1-1 without prescribing any other period, so this is the Norwegian day/night VFR boundary. Note: it is NOT sunset — at our latitudes civil twilight can last from ~30 minutes to many hours past sunset.
  • With an ETD set, every takeoff and landing in the mission — intermediate stops included — is checked at its own aerodrome on its own calendar date: red = outside day VFR (night per SERA), amber = landing within a 30-minute planning margin of the window's end (the margin is a suggestion, not a rule). Intermediate stops get their own STOP row on the card.
  • Polar cases are handled: midnight sun / bright summer nights (day VFR all 24 h), polar-night days where the sun never rises but a legal twilight window still exists, and deep polar night (no day-VFR window at all — the card says so instead of showing meaningless times).
  • Times are local (this computer's timezone — the card states the UTC offset for the flight date, DST included). ETD is local clock time too. AIP and almanac tables are usually UTC: add the local offset when cross-checking.
  • Accuracy: NOAA solar equations, cross-checked against US Naval Observatory almanac data (±2 min at 69°N; degrades above 72° latitude — the card warns). The computed times are for planning; the official times in AIP Norge GEN 2.7 / the almanac govern. Checking legality of the actual flight remains PIC responsibility.

🗺 Map Tools

  • 📏 Ruler: click points to measure true & magnetic track, leg and total distance — each segment gets a chip on the map (MT · distance, hover for true track) plus a running-total chip at the last point, and the banner shows the full detail.
  • TOC/TOD labels include the distance from a named waypoint (e.g. "▲ TOC 2500' · 3.8 NM after ENDU") so you can mark them with a ruler on your chart. Out-and-back legs place their labels on opposite sides of the line.
  • Climbs and descents are planned across leg boundaries: a climb that doesn't fit its leg simply continues on the next (the ▲ TOC lands on the leg where the altitude is actually reached), and a descent that doesn't fit its leg backs up onto earlier legs — the ▼ TOD names the low waypoint it serves (e.g. "TOD · 7.2 NM before B → 200' at C") — so you always cross a waypoint AT its planned altitude, never above it. A circuit (pattern) stop breaks the chain. If even all the preceding distance can't absorb the descent, the red integrity banner says so instead of letting you plan an arrival that's too high.
  • Multiple flight plans draw in different colours; every second one is dashed so overlapping routes stay readable.
  • The 🗺 Chart button switches the base map between Kartverket topo and the official ICAO VFR 1:500 000 chart, streamed from Avinor's public map service. Both charts need internet — neither is stored on your machine. The paper VFR chart is Lambert conformal conic (EUREF89, 59°40′/69°20′), but Avinor's service reprojects it server-side to Web Mercator — the same projection as the topo tiles — so waypoints sit on exactly the same spot on both charts; switching can never move a point. The small label at the bottom-left of the map always states which chart and projection you are looking at, including the chart's AIRAC edition when online. The choice is remembered with your settings.
  • ⬢ Airspace draws the published airspace from AIP Norge — CTR and TIZ solid, TMA and TIA dashed, CTA finely dashed, the mandatory zones (RMZ/TMZ) in orange. Hover any airspace for a card showing its type, ICAO class, published vertical limits, and one row per service you would actually use — ATIS, Approach, Tower, or Information at an AFIS field. Nothing is drawn below zoom 7: at country zoom the polygons bury the chart you are trying to read. Clicking still adds a waypoint, so route building inside a TMA is unaffected — the information is on hover only. The choice is remembered with your settings.
  • Right-click the track for that leg's settings: the altitude it is crossed at, and where its climb and descent sit. Three corners are yours to name — start the climb N NM after the fix (hold your current altitude, then go up), be level by N NM after it (which sets the start of the climb for you), and level off N NM before the next fix (get down early, then run in level). Both are pure geometry: the climb and descent are unchanged, they just move, so they are always flyable. The Here buttons turn the spot you right-clicked into the number. A live preview shows the leg's time, fuel and where each corner lands before you apply anything, and the pinned corners are drawn on the map as BOC and BOD marks beside the usual TOC and TOD. Right-clicking used to insert a waypoint outright; that is now the first action in the panel, at the exact point you clicked, so nothing was lost.
  • "Be level by" names the top of climb, and the bottom is worked backwards from it — at your profile's own rate, so the climb's time, fuel and TAS are never invented; only its position moves. Ask to be level 20 NM out and the planner simply starts the climb later. Ask for something the climb cannot reach even starting from the leg's first fix and it tells you what altitude the previous fix would have to be crossed at — always a whole hundred feet, rounded up, because the figure is a minimum and the nearest hundred would be below it half the time — with a button to set it — because that keeps the altitude column honest about what is actually flown where, instead of the schedule quietly doing something the column denies. Taking it gives you one continuous climb: the earlier leg's climb is delayed so it tops out exactly on that fix and runs straight on, rather than levelling off there and climbing again. The rounding buys you a little height, so you top out slightly before the distance you asked for rather than exactly on it — "be level by" is a deadline, and early is safe. Every such suggestion is tried before it is offered: raising a fix also changes the leg before it, and if those earlier legs cannot climb that high by then, the panel says so rather than giving advice that does not work. On the very first leg there is no earlier fix to raise — you cannot climb before takeoff — so there the only honest answer is the rate it would need, and you get it. Nothing steeper than your profile is ever flown. A level-off that would fall inside a descent still running to a later, lower fix is likewise refused and named, in the panel and the red banner, never quietly ignored.
  • ⌖ Fixes draws the published aerodromes (from zoom 7) and VFR reporting points (from zoom 9) from AIP Norge. Click one and it becomes a waypoint at its published coordinate — no dialog, because the fix already has its published name — so a leg to SØRREISA is drawn to the point the chart states, not to wherever you managed to click. An aerodrome also brings its published field elevation, and as the first waypoint it sets the flight's departure elevation to it. Hover any fix for its published DMS. The symbols are yours to set — Settings → Map has the shape, colour, size, fill style and whether names are shown, with a live preview. Reporting points default to orange because nothing on either base chart is orange except the mandatory zones, so the symbol you are hunting for cannot be mistaken for published chart ink. 🔎 Find fix searches by name or ICAO; the Norwegian letters are optional, so SORKJOSEN finds SØRKJOSEN, and matches are ordered by strength then by distance from the middle of your map. Clicking bare map still adds a waypoint the old way — the fixes are 9-pixel symbols and take only their own clicks. The choice is remembered with your settings.
  • What the fixes layer will not do. Reporting points come from the coordinate table printed on each aerodrome's Visual Approach Chart. 29 of the 53 aerodromes publish their points on the chart face only, with no table to read — those aerodromes are still here and still anchor on their ARP, but they carry no points, and nothing is taken off a chart image. 243 points at 24 aerodromes is the whole of what the current edition states in machine-readable form; the VAC remains the reference for the rest. Every coordinate is checked against the chart's own printed lat/long graticule before it ships, and names that fail to decode are refused rather than shipped misspelt. The chart raster itself is not georeferenced, so there is no VAC overlay. A planning aid — verify against the current AIP and NOTAM.
  • What the airspace layer will not do. Vertical limits are shown exactly as published — GND, UNL and flight levels stay as they are, and a flight level is never converted to an altitude. Where the source states no class, the card says so rather than guessing. Airspaces whose published boundary cannot be resolved are absent rather than approximated: the offshore zones defined as a circle radius, the maritime stretch of the Norway–Sweden boundary in the Skagerrak, and one citing the Finland–Sweden border. Where the AIP does say a boundary follows the national border, the real border is drawn from Kartverket’s official line — 51 such stretches in this edition, on 41 airspaces. ATS delegation areas are not drawn at all, and that is deliberate: ENR 2.2 section 5 publishes the areas where two states have agreed to transfer who provides the service, not the airspace itself. Silver 1 and Silver 2 are inside SWEDEN FIR, Halti and Manto inside HELSINKI FIR — 13 of the 17 lie in a foreign FIR, and drawing them as class-C volumes made them look like controlled airspace you had to clear. The card lists only the frequencies you would dial: clearance delivery and ground are left out, military channels are excluded (both the flagged ones and everything outside the 118–137 MHz civil band), and 121.500 is never listed — you know it. An airspace worked only by an area control centre still shows that frequency, because otherwise there would be nobody to call. Polaris CTA is a special case: it is one airspace over the whole country, so the AIP lists all 26 sector frequencies against it. The card therefore names the sector your cursor is in — ENR 2.2 publishes each Polaris sector as its own volume, so this is a lookup, not a guess, and the frequency shown is always one the hovered airspace itself publishes. Where the AIP stacks sectors vertically you get one row per sector, each labelled with the band it works, so you pick by planned level. Over the Oslofjord, where Sectors 3 and 4 cannot be drawn (their boundary follows the maritime Norway–Sweden line, which is not in Kartverket’s land-border data), the card says so and shows no frequency rather than reciting all 26. ATIS rows are labelled <ICAO> ATIS rather than by the published callsign, which in Norway is often "…Information" and reads exactly like the AFIS service you would actually talk to. Every published service and frequency remains in the data. This is a planning aid — verify against the current AIP and NOTAM.
  • Zooming out declutters automatically: at region zoom the waypoint labels compact and the TOC/TOD chips hide (the tick across the track stays); zoomed further out only waypoint dots and route lines stay. Zoom back in and everything returns — nothing is lost, it's display-only. The 🔎 Declutter button on the map changes this: Auto follows the zoom, or lock Full / Mid / Far regardless of zoom. The choice is remembered with your settings.

🧭 Chart Plotting & OFP

  • Expand the 🗺 Chart Plotting List under each flight: waypoint coordinates in degrees + decimal minutes (as on chart margins), editable names, and whole-leg TT/MT/distance — the lines you actually draw.
  • 📋 Copy List puts it on your clipboard as aligned text.
  • Print OFP prints the company "Operational flightplan" form (page 1 of C182OFPMBv4.2), filled in from your plan — one sheet per sector, and a second sheet when a flight has more than the form's 16 lines. It is landscape A4.
    • What is filled: From/To, TAS, TT, VAR, MT, wind, WCA, accumulated distance & time, FF, leg and accumulated fuel, PL, MH, GS, leg distance & time, ETO, estimated fuel remaining, the Total line, the date and the departure fuel.
    • What is deliberately left blank, because filling it would be a guess: MSA (this tool has no terrain data — read it off your chart), ATO / Diff / ACT and the block, take-off and landing times (those are actuals you record in flight), Freq (AIP frequencies belong to airspace, not to a leg), the alternate line, and Reg, CREW, PASSENGERS and PIC — the planner does not hold crew names or a tail number, and it never will.
    • The Mass & Balance side of the form is not reproduced yet.
  • ➕ Add Next Flight Plan chains sectors (touch & go / next hop); fuel and time accumulate across the whole mission.

🖥 Layout & Display

  • The view selector offers Split, Stacked, Plan-only and Map-only — Stacked/Plan give the OFP the full window width on laptops.
  • ▤ Compact hides secondary columns, leaving the flying essentials. Both choices are remembered per browser.

💾 Saving, Export & Import — important

  • Work is auto-saved in this browser's storage — it survives restarts, but it does NOT live inside the HTML file and won't follow you to another browser or computer.
  • Save stores a named route or whole mission; the dropdown loads them back.
  • Export writes a JSON file with everything: routes, missions, current flights, aircraft settings and planning preferences. Import restores it anywhere. Export after any session you'd mind losing.

🔢 Version & updates

  • The version badge next to the title shows which version you are running and what the update check found: latest (hh:mm) with the time it checked, a yellow ⬆ available link when a newer release exists, or update check failed when GitHub could not be reached. Click the underlined part to check again at any time. The check never blocks anything — the planner works the same offline.

📡 Offline

  • The planner works with no internet; the map does not. Waypoints, tracks, distances, headings, fuel and times all keep working — only the background chart goes blank, and it reloads automatically when the connection returns. Winds aloft, METAR/TAF and the update check need a connection too.
  • Opened from the hosted address, the app itself is stored by your browser after the first visit and opens offline. The double-click file needs nothing at all — it is one self-contained file.
  • There is no chart download, on purpose. A version of this existed and was removed: Avinor's chart tiles come without the header a browser needs to measure them, so the browser charges megabytes of storage for each one whatever its real size. A route's worth filled the storage allowance, and the browser then threw away everything for the site — the chart worked for a while and then vanished. A button promising an offline chart that quietly loses it is worse than no button. Plan on the paper chart being your offline chart.

Zoom thresholds — fixed, and why

Aerodromes appear from zoom 7, reporting points from 9, and airspace from 7. These are not adjustable on purpose: at country zoom the 243 reporting points are a spatter that hides the 53 aerodromes, and 227 airspace polygons are a wash that hides the CTRs that matter.
